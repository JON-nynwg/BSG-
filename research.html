WHERE WE FOCUS

Our research priorities.

These priorities are informed by the World Health Organization's own findings on the global epilepsy treatment gap.

Closing the Treatment Gap

In low-income countries, up to three-quarters of people with epilepsy still go without the treatment they need — often despite low-cost medication being available.

Community Health Worker Training

Most people with epilepsy can be diagnosed and treated at the primary care level — if health workers have the right training.

Stigma-Reduction Interventions

Understanding which awareness approaches actually change attitudes and behavior toward people with epilepsy.

Access to Affordable Medicines

Antiseizure medicines can cost as little as a few dollars a year, yet availability in the public sector of many low- and middle-income countries remains inconsistent.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

Working within a global framework.

Our research and advocacy priorities are informed by international frameworks such as the World Health Organization's Intersectoral Global Action Plan on Epilepsy and Other Neurological Disorders 2022–2031 (IGAP), adopted by all WHO member states to improve diagnosis, treatment and public health responses to epilepsy worldwide. We look to align our local programs in Kenya with this global direction, and we welcome conversations with researchers, clinicians and institutions who share these goals.
