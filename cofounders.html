CO-FOUNDERS

Shared vision.
Shared leadership.

Beyond Seizure Global is built by a small, committed circle of co-founders, each bringing a different strength to the same mission: educate, empower and end the stigma around epilepsy.

Erick O. Orangi

Community Neuro-Health Nurse

  • RN
  • Co-founder

Neurology is usually described in scans and prescriptions, but a condition of the nervous system is lived in rhythms: the pill that has to be taken at the same hour, the night of broken sleep, the staircase at home, the employer who has never heard the word. Erick, a registered nurse in the United States, spends the working week inside those rhythms, in the neighbourhood rather than the ward.

That vantage point gives BSG its sense of proportion. A guideline is only as good as its performance on a bad Wednesday in a crowded house. Erick is the colleague who asks of every idea whether it survives that test.

Why it matters

The hospital sees the worst hour of an illness. The neighbourhood sees every other hour, and most of a life is made of those.

What Erick brings to BSG

  • A habit of testing every idea against ordinary daily life
  • Clinical judgment from a registered nurse
  • A base in the United States for partnerships and learning

Gaudencia B. M.

Patient Navigator & Epilepsy Advocate

  • RN
  • Co-founder

A prescription is a promise written on paper. Between that paper and the medicine in a person's hand lie referrals, queues, transport fares, laboratory slips and a pharmacy that may or may not have stock. Gaudencia, a Kenyan registered nurse, has built a working life around that stretch of road, walking it beside people who would otherwise walk it alone.

Nurses learn to read a chart. Gaudencia reads the situation around the chart as well. Every obstacle a family meets becomes evidence, and evidence is what changes minds in clinics, offices and communities. That is the second half of the work: turning one family's trouble into a case nobody can wave away.

Why it matters

Most people do not leave treatment because they gave up. They slip out somewhere between two appointments, where nobody was watching. The work is to be standing in that gap.

What Gaudencia brings to BSG

  • The clinical knowledge to explain a plan and the street-level knowledge to make it workable
  • A map of the places where the health system loses people
  • A Kenyan viewpoint at the centre of BSG's decisions

Elisha Mwangi

Seizure First Aid Educator

  • RN
  • Co-founder

Fear does more harm during a seizure than most people realise, because frightened onlookers reach for the wrong things: a spoon, a tight hold, a stranger's loud advice. Elisha, a Kenyan registered nurse, works on the minutes that matter most, teaching ordinary people to swap panic for a routine they can still recall under pressure.

The steps are deliberately few. Watch the clock, clear the space, turn the person gently onto their side, stay close, and know when to call for help. A room of twenty people who have learned them is twenty people who will not freeze the next time it happens.

Why it matters

Medicine cannot be present at every seizure. A trained neighbour can. Teaching one person quietly places a safety net under everyone they will ever stand beside.

What Elisha brings to BSG

  • A nurse's accuracy, delivered in language anyone can follow
  • Teaching that turns hesitation into action
  • Credibility with schools, families and workplaces

Kaith brings engineering discipline, multilingual communication and hands-on epilepsy advocacy to advance BSG's mission, helping accurate information and support reach people across languages and borders.

HOW WE LEAD

Educate

Plain, accurate information about epilepsy for families, schools and communities.

Empower

Programs and peer support that help people living with epilepsy speak up and be heard.

End Stigma

Awareness campaigns and research that push back on silence, fear and misinformation.
